# Security and Architecture Questions to Ask

:::objectives
- Carry a repertoire of questions that reveal how a client's environment really works and where the design must adapt.
- Ask them in a way that elicits honest answers rather than policy recitations.
- Turn answers into design constraints, risks and requirements.
:::

:::prereq
[Technical Discovery: Understanding a Client's Infrastructure](#/p13/c02); Part IV.
:::

## How to ask

Three habits make the answers useful. First, ask for the **mechanism** ("how does a developer get a database password today?") rather than the policy ("do you have a secrets policy?"); mechanisms reveal truth. Second, ask about **the last time** ("when did you last restore a backup? what happened?"); specifics beat generalities. Third, ask **what would happen if** ("if this server disappeared tonight, what would you do?"); hypotheticals surface untested assumptions without accusing anyone. Write down the answer and the person; follow up where answers conflict.

## Identity and access

- How do staff authenticate to internal systems? Is MFA enforced for everyone, including admins and service accounts? Any exceptions?
- How will our system authenticate your users: OIDC, SAML, both? Which groups or claims carry roles? Who manages group membership?
- How are service-to-service credentials issued, stored and rotated today? Where would ours live?
- Who has administrative access to the cloud accounts we will use? How is it granted, reviewed and removed? When was the last access review?
- How do contractors and former employees lose access? How fast?
- Is there a privileged access management or just-in-time elevation tool we must use?
- How will our team get access, and how long does it take? Can it be time-bound and read-only by default?

## Network and connectivity

- From the subnets where we will run, what can reach the internet, and through what (NAT, proxy, allowlist)? Who approves a new destination and how long does it take?
- What inbound paths exist (load balancers, WAF, API gateway)? Who owns them? Can we add listeners and rules, or must you?
- How does traffic reach your on-premises systems from the cloud? Bandwidth, redundancy, latency?
- Which DNS zones will we use, who edits them, and how fast do changes propagate?
- Is traffic between internal services encrypted? Is that required of us?
- How would you know if an attacker were inside the network? (Detection capability, honestly described.)

## Data

- Which data will our system hold or process? Who owns each dataset? What is its classification?
- Is any of it personal, financial, health or otherwise regulated? Which jurisdictions? Any residency requirements for storage, processing and backups?
- Retention requirements and deletion obligations? How do you handle a subject access or erasure request today?
- May we use production data for testing? (The answer must be no; ask to see how they react.)
- Who may see this data in production? Will your support staff need access to our system's data? Through what controls?
- Where do logs go, who can read them, and how long are they kept? Any restrictions on what may appear in logs?

## Security controls and compliance

- Which security policies and standards apply to systems like ours? Can we have them in writing?
- What must be true before go-live: security review, penetration test, questionnaire, architecture board approval? Who runs them and how long do they take?
- Which security tooling must we integrate with: SIEM (what format and transport), EDR, vulnerability scanners, secret manager, certificate authority?
- What is your vulnerability SLA by severity? How will you expect us to report and remediate?
- Which compliance regimes apply (SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI, HIPAA, GDPR, sector rules)? What evidence will auditors ask us for?
- Have you had a security incident involving a vendor system? What changed afterwards?
- Who is the security contact for incidents, and what is the notification expectation?

## Architecture and platform

- Which cloud services and regions are approved? Is there an approval path for others and how long does it take?
- Do you have a landing zone, account structure and guardrails we must fit? Can we see the policies?
- Is infrastructure as code mandated? Which tool? Where does state live? Who runs applies?
- Which CI/CD platform may we use? Can it reach your cloud accounts? Through what identity?
- What are your standards for containers, base images, registries, Kubernetes (if any)?
- How are secrets provided to running applications in your environment today?
- What observability platform must we feed? What dashboards and alerts do your operators expect?
- What are your backup and DR standards? RTO/RPO for a system of this criticality?
- What breaks most often in your environment? What do you wish every vendor knew before deploying here?

## Operations and process

- Who will operate this system after go-live, with what skills and hours? What do they need from us?
- What is the change process? Windows, freezes, approvals, lead times? What qualifies as a standard change?
- How are incidents declared, run and communicated here? Severity definitions? Will we be in your incident process or you in ours?
- How do you handle patching and upgrades for vendor systems today? Who schedules them?
- How do you monitor costs? Who sees the bill for our resources? Budget alarms?

## Integration specifics

For each system we must integrate with: who owns it; the interface (API, files, queue), its documentation and version; authentication method; a test environment and how to get access; rate limits and availability expectations; data formats and quirks; change notice (how will we know when it changes); historical reliability; whether anyone else integrates with it successfully today.

## Turning answers into design

Each answer produces one of: a **constraint** (must use Entra ID via SAML; no internet egress without proxy), a **requirement** (ship logs to Splunk over HEC; RPO 1 hour), a **risk** (no MFA on cloud root; backups never restored; see chapter 4), an **assumption to verify** (API supports idempotency), or a **lead-time item** (firewall rule, 10 business days). Tag answers as you record them; the design document's constraints section and the risk register write themselves.

:::fde The question behind the questions
You are also assessing whether the client's environment can safely host what you are about to build, and whether their organisation can operate it. If the answers reveal that nobody owns identity, backups have never been tested and changes happen in the console, the honest design includes fixing enough of that first, or scoping your deployment to minimise dependence on it, and saying so in writing.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Accepting policy statements as descriptions of reality.
- Asking yes/no questions that invite reassurance.
- Not asking about lead times.
- Forgetting the operators' questions (who runs it, with what hours).
- Not asking about previous vendor incidents and what changed.
- Collecting answers without converting them into constraints and risks.

## Exercises

1. Pick fifteen questions from this chapter most relevant to your product and rewrite them as mechanism, last-time or what-if questions.
2. Interview a colleague role-playing a client with a messy environment; tag each answer as constraint, requirement, risk, assumption or lead time.
3. For one integration you know, write the integration-specific question set and answer it from available documentation; list what remains unknown.
