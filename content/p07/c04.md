# Billing, Tagging, Budgets and Cost Monitoring

:::objectives
- Read an AWS bill and attribute cost to services, environments and clients.
- Implement cost allocation tags, budgets and anomaly alerts so surprises are caught in hours, not at month end.
- Recognise the recurring surprise charges and design to avoid them.
- Estimate and present costs to clients honestly, including the cost of operating.
:::

:::prereq
Chapters 1 and 2; Part V chapter 1.
:::

## The problem

Cloud bills are itemised to the second across hundreds of line items, and nobody reads them until one is shocking. The two classic failures: a client receives a bill several times the estimate because of charges the estimate omitted (data transfer, NAT, load balancers, logs), or nobody notices a runaway resource (a forgotten instance, an infinite Lambda loop, a misconfigured log group) until it has cost thousands. Cost is an engineering property like latency: measured, attributed, alerted on and designed for.

## How the bill is structured

Charges accrue per **account**, per **service**, per **usage type** (e.g. `EUW1-BoxUsage:t3.medium`, `EUW1-DataTransfer-Out-Bytes`, `EUW1-NatGateway-Hours`), per **Region**, and are visible in **Cost Explorer** (interactive, by day/month, filterable and groupable by service, account, tag, usage type), the **Cost and Usage Report** (hourly detail to S3 for Athena/QuickSight analysis), and **Billing** (invoices, credits, free tier usage). With Organizations, the management account sees everything consolidated; member accounts see their own.

Monthly rhythm: on the first working day, review last month by service and account against the estimate; investigate anything over 10% off; note trends. Weekly: glance at the daily cost graph for steps.

## Tagging for attribution

A tag is a key-value label on a resource. **Cost allocation tags** (activated in Billing) make tags available as dimensions in Cost Explorer and reports. Without them, you know that "EC2 cost $2,400" but not which client or environment.

A minimal tagging standard, applied by IaC to every taggable resource:

| Tag | Values | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `Environment` | `prod`, `staging`, `dev`, `sandbox` | Separate environment costs; policy conditions |
| `Project` / `Client` | `client-a`, `internal-platform` | Attribution and chargeback |
| `Service` | `api`, `worker`, `frontend`, `shared` | Which component |
| `Owner` | team or email | Who to ask |
| `CostCenter` | client's code | Their finance mapping |
| `ManagedBy` | `terraform`, `console` | Find console-created drift |

Enforce with: default tags in the Terraform provider (`default_tags`), AWS Config rule `required-tags`, SCPs that deny creating certain resources without tags (`aws:RequestTag` conditions), and Tag Policies in Organizations. Some costs cannot be tagged (data transfer, some support fees); use account separation to attribute those.

## Budgets and alerts

**AWS Budgets**: monthly cost budgets per account and per tag with alerts at thresholds (50/80/100% actual, and *forecasted* 100%), delivered to SNS/email/Slack. Also usage budgets (hours of a resource type) and RI/Savings Plan coverage budgets. Set one per environment and per client at minimum.

**Cost Anomaly Detection**: machine-learned baselines per service/account/tag with alerts on deviations; catches the runaway resource in a day rather than a month. Enable it with a monitor per account and an alert threshold (e.g., anomalies over $50).

**CloudWatch billing alarms** (us-east-1 only, on `EstimatedCharges`): the oldest mechanism; still worth one alarm per account as a backstop.

**Free tier alerts**: enable so learners and sandboxes get warned.

| Charge | Why it surprises | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **NAT Gateway** hourly + per GB | $32+/month per AZ before traffic; GB charges on every byte to S3, ECR, updates | Gateway endpoints for S3/DynamoDB (free); interface endpoints where volume justifies; one NAT for non-prod; IPv6 egress-only gateway |
| **Data transfer out** to Internet | $0.09/GB after 100 GB free; media and API-heavy apps add up | CloudFront (cheaper egress, free tier); compression; caching |
| **Cross-AZ transfer** | $0.01/GB each way between AZs, including replication and chatty services | Topology awareness; keep chatty pairs in one AZ when safe; accept for HA |
| **Load balancers** | ~$16–25/month each before traffic; one per service multiplies | Share ALBs with host/path rules |
| **CloudWatch Logs ingestion** | $0.50/GB ingested; debug logging at scale costs more than the compute | Log levels; sampling; retention; metric filters instead of log queries |
| **EBS volumes and snapshots** | Unattached volumes and forgotten snapshots bill forever | Lifecycle policies; tag and audit; delete on terminate |
| **Idle resources** | Dev instances running nights and weekends; forgotten RDS; old Elastic IPs (charged when unattached, and now all public IPv4 addresses carry an hourly charge) | Schedulers (Instance Scheduler, Lambda cron); budgets per sandbox; automated cleanup |
| **Public IPv4 addresses** | ~$3.60/month each since 2024, including those on instances and NAT gateways | Private subnets; fewer public IPs; IPv6 |
| **Secrets Manager** | $0.40/secret/month adds up with hundreds of secrets | Parameter Store standard tier for low-churn config |
| **Lambda in a loop** | A recursive trigger (S3 event → Lambda → writes to S3) runs millions of times | Recursion detection (now built-in for some patterns); concurrency limits; budgets |
| **Over-provisioned databases** | db.r6g.xlarge for 50 users | Right-size from metrics; Graviton; serverless v2 for spiky loads |
| **Support plan** | Business support is 10% of the bill (min $100) | Choose deliberately; Developer tier for non-production |

Illustrative prices are for us-east-1 at the time of writing; always confirm in the AWS Pricing Calculator.

## Estimating for a client

Build the estimate bottom-up from the architecture diagram: every box and every arrow has a price. Include the unglamorous lines (NAT, ALB, logs, backups, data transfer, secrets, DNS, certificates (free with ACM), support). Add a range (expected and worst-case, e.g. 3× traffic). State assumptions (traffic, data volume, retention). Add the **people cost**: hours per month to operate (patching, upgrades, on-call) at the client's or your rate; this is usually larger than the AWS bill for small systems and is the honest argument for managed services. Present monthly and three-year totals. Revisit after the first month of real usage.

```text
Example: small production SaaS (illustrative, eu-west-1)
ECS Fargate 2×(0.5 vCPU, 1 GB) always on ........ ~$40
ALB ................................................. ~$22
NAT Gateway (1 AZ) + 100 GB .......................... ~$37
RDS db.t4g.small Multi-AZ + 50 GB gp3 ................ ~$70
ElastiCache t4g.micro ................................ ~$13
S3 50 GB + requests .................................. ~$3
CloudWatch logs 10 GB + alarms + metrics ............. ~$10
Secrets Manager 6 secrets ............................ ~$2.4
Route 53 zone + queries .............................. ~$1
Data transfer out 100 GB (after free) ................ ~$9
Backups to second Region ............................. ~$5
                                             Total ≈ $210/month, before support and people time
Multi-AZ NAT (+$32), bigger RDS, WAF (+$10+), more logs: easily $300–400.
```

## Reducing cost responsibly

In order of impact for typical client systems: turn off what is idle (schedules for non-prod; delete unattached storage); right-size from metrics (CPU under 20% for a month means a smaller instance or Fargate task); use Graviton; move static and cacheable traffic to CloudFront; replace NAT-routed traffic to AWS services with endpoints; set log retention and levels; commit to Savings Plans only for the stable baseline after three months of data; use Spot for stateless workers and CI; review monthly. Never cut Multi-AZ on production databases or backups to save money without the client's explicit, written acceptance of the risk.

## Governance

Budgets per account; anomaly detection on; tagging enforced; monthly review with the client showing cost by component versus estimate; a "cost" line in every architecture decision record; sandbox accounts with hard budgets and automated teardown. For client-owned accounts, agree who receives alerts and who may approve spend above thresholds (Part XIII chapter 5).

## Common mistakes

- Estimates that include only compute and database.
- No budgets or anomaly alerts; the first signal is the invoice.
- Untagged resources; cost attribution impossible across clients.
- NAT gateways in three AZs for a staging environment with no traffic.
- CloudWatch Logs at DEBUG with infinite retention.
- Reserved purchases before measuring; or never purchasing for a stable three-year workload.
- Idle dev environments running 24×7.
- Presenting AWS cost without operations cost to a client choosing between options.
