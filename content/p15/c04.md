# Incident Response Guide

:::objectives
- Provide a compact guide to follow during an incident: declaration, roles, severity, the response loop, communication templates, security-specific steps and closure.
- Be usable at 03:00 by someone who has read it once before.
:::

:::prereq
[On-Call and Incident Response](#/p11/c06); [Root Cause Analysis and Blameless Postmortems](#/p11/c07); [Client Handovers, Operational Ownership and Professional Incident Management](#/p12/c10).
:::

## Quick card

```checklist
# Incident quick card
- Declare: create the incident (channel/bridge/ticket) the moment impact is suspected; declaring is free
- Assign: incident commander (IC), hands-on lead, communications lead, scribe; one person may hold several roles in a small incident but names them
- Assess: user impact, start time, what changed; set severity (table below); adjust later
- Mitigate first: roll back, fail over, flag off, scale, block; reversible actions before diagnosis
- One change at a time; record every action with a timestamp
- Communicate on cadence: internal every 15–30 min; status page per severity; next update time in every message
- Verify from the user's side: SLIs, probes, support confirmation
- Preserve evidence before destructive fixes (snapshots, logs)
- Close: final update, timeline saved, postmortem scheduled within 48 h, responders rest
```

## Severity

| Severity | Impact | Response | External communication |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEV1 | Critical functions down for most users; data loss; active security breach | All hands; IC; leadership paged; 24×7 | Status page within 15 min; updates every 30 min; client security contact for breaches |
| SEV2 | Major degradation or key feature down for many users | On-call plus owners; IC | Status page; hourly updates |
| SEV3 | Minor impairment or small subset of users | On-call in business hours | Inform affected customers if asked |
| SEV4 | Cosmetic or internal only | Ticket | None |

When in doubt, declare higher and downgrade.

## Roles

- **Incident Commander**: owns the incident; decides priorities; assigns actions with owners and times; runs the loop every 10–15 minutes (what do we know, what are we doing, who is doing it, when do we check back); does not debug.
- **Hands-on lead**: directs technical investigation and mitigation; reports to the IC.
- **Communications lead**: writes internal and external updates; handles stakeholders and support; protects the responders from interruptions.
- **Scribe**: maintains the timeline (UTC timestamps, observations, actions, decisions); collects links to dashboards and logs.
- **Client liaison** (client incidents): the single voice to the client; obtains client decisions on business trade-offs.

## The response loop

```flow
{
  "title": "Response loop",
  "participants": ["Impact","Timeline","Change","Layer","Hypothesis","Mitigate","Verify"],
  "steps": [
    {"from":"Impact","to":"Timeline","label":"Who, what, since when","detail":"Get a concrete example (request ID, user, time) and the SLI graph. Severity from impact."},
    {"from":"Timeline","to":"Change","label":"Exact start time","detail":"From dashboards. Everything that changed near it is a suspect: deploys, config, infrastructure, secrets, certificates, DNS, vendor maintenance, traffic, scheduled jobs."},
    {"from":"Change","to":"Mitigate","label":"A change matches? Revert it","detail":"Roll back first, understand later."},
    {"from":"Change","to":"Layer","label":"No obvious change","detail":"Walk the request path: client → DNS → edge → LB → app → dependencies. Find the first hop where good becomes bad."},
    {"from":"Layer","to":"Hypothesis","label":"Most likely cause at that layer","detail":"State it, name the observation that confirms or refutes it, make the observation. One at a time."},
    {"from":"Hypothesis","to":"Mitigate","label":"Confirmed","detail":"Smallest reversible action that restores service: scale, fail over, flag off, block, restart a specific component, kill a specific query."},
    {"from":"Mitigate","to":"Verify","label":"Applied","detail":"SLIs recover; probes green; a user-side check succeeds. If not, revert the mitigation and return to Hypothesis."}
  ]
}
```

## Communication templates

Internal (every 15–30 minutes):

```text
[10:15 UTC] SEV2 — portal sign-in errors
Impact: ~30% of sign-in attempts failing since 09:52. Other features OK.
What we know: errors are 502 from the auth service; started 4 minutes after deploy 5.12.
Actions: rolling back to 5.11 (owner: A, ETA 10:22). Investigating connection pool (owner: B).
Next update: 10:35 UTC.
```

Status page:

```text
Investigating — We are investigating errors affecting sign-in for some customers. Other features are operating normally. Next update by [time].
Identified — We have identified the cause as a recent change and are rolling it back. Next update by [time].
Monitoring — A fix has been applied and sign-in is recovering. We are monitoring. Next update by [time].
Resolved — Service was fully restored at [time]. The issue lasted approximately [N] minutes. A postmortem will follow within [N] business days.
```

Client notification (contracted incidents):

```text
Subject: [SEV2] Portal sign-in errors — initial notification
We are aware of sign-in failures affecting a portion of your users since 09:52 UTC. Our team is responding; initial mitigation (rollback of a recent change) is under way. No indication of data loss or security impact at this time. Next update by 10:35 UTC. Incident reference: INC-2026-1101-01. Contact: [liaison].
```

Rules: impact, actions, next update time; no speculation about cause; no blame; no "small number of users" unless you know; same reference ID everywhere.

## Security incident additions

```checklist
# Security incident steps (in addition to the loop)
- Declare SEV1 for confirmed or suspected breach; engage the security lead and legal; notify the client's security contact per contract (often within hours)
- Preserve evidence first: snapshot affected instances/volumes, export logs, record indicators; do not terminate or wipe
- Contain: revoke compromised sessions and credentials; isolate affected hosts (security group with no egress); disable compromised accounts; block attacker indicators at the edge
- Rotate: every credential the compromised identity could reach; signing keys if token issuance may be affected (force re-authentication)
- Re-enable and verify logging if it was disabled; check organisation-level trails for the attacker's actions
- Scope: what data was accessed (object-level logs, query logs); which systems; over what period
- Eradicate: fix the entry point (patch, configuration, code); verify no persistence (new users, keys, roles, scheduled tasks, modified images)
- Recover: restore from clean backups where integrity is in doubt; monitor for recurrence
- Notify: regulators and data subjects per obligations and timelines (GDPR 72 h from awareness); coordinate wording with legal and the client; never go external without agreement
- Document: forensic timeline, decisions, who was told what when; retain for the legal horizon
```

## Closure

- Final update internally and externally with duration and impact figures.
- Timeline exported and attached; dashboards snapshotted; logs retained beyond normal rotation.
- Postmortem scheduled within 48 hours with responders, owners and a facilitator (Part XI chapter 7); client report within the contracted period.
- Responders stand down; time off after night incidents.
- Action items tracked; alerts and runbooks updated with what was learned.

## Common failures of incident response

Debugging before mitigating; no IC; several people changing things at once; silence toward stakeholders; declaring only total outages; destroying evidence in a security incident; promising a cause before knowing it; skipping the postmortem.
