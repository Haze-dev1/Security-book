# Client Project Handover Checklist

:::objectives
- Provide the acceptance checklist for handing a production system to a client or receiving team, covering documentation, access, operations, security, cost and knowledge transfer.
- Make handover completion a demonstrated, signed fact rather than an assumption.
:::

:::prereq
[Client Handovers, Operational Ownership and Professional Incident Management](#/p12/c10); [Technical Handovers](#/p13/c12).
:::

## How to use this checklist

Start six weeks before the engagement ends. Each item is complete when the receiving team confirms it, with evidence linked; the final section is signed by both sides. Items the receiving team cannot yet demonstrate are either completed before sign-off or recorded as accepted risks with a remediation plan. Your checklist state persists in this browser; export notes into the handover record.

```checklist
# Client Project Handover Checklist
## 1. Ownership and support
- Receiving owner named (team, not individual) with manager and escalation contact
- Service ownership record completed: criticality, SLOs, on-call, authority, dependencies
- Support model agreed in writing: hours, severities, response and resolution targets, what is in and out of scope, cost of out-of-scope work
- Incident process agreed: who declares, how to reach each party, communication expectations, client decision-makers for business trade-offs
- Shared-responsibility RACI covering patching, secrets rotation, monitoring, incident response, backups, changes, cost, compliance evidence
- Hypercare period defined: duration, response times, weekly review, exit criteria
## 2. Documentation delivered (in the client's repository or wiki)
- README with local run, test and layout
- Architecture overview with diagrams-as-code, trust boundaries, integrations; ADRs for significant decisions
- Environment document per environment with last-verified dates
- Configuration reference and client manifest; every key documented with owner and change procedure
- Operations runbooks: deploy, rollback, scale, rotate each secret, restore, rebuild; one per alert
- On-call guide: schedule, escalation, tools, dashboards, severity definitions
- API documentation (OpenAPI) and integration contracts with owners and contacts
- Data map: data stores, classification, personal data, retention, processors, deletion and export procedures
- Risk register and technical debt register with recommendations and priorities, accepted by the client
- Version matrix with end-of-life dates and upgrade recommendations
- Maintenance calendar: patching, dependency updates, certificate renewals, access reviews, restore drills, DR tests; each with a client owner
- Cost report: current monthly spend by component, budget alarms, reserved commitments and expiry, forecast
- Release notes and changelog to date
## 3. Code, infrastructure and pipelines
- Source and IaC repositories owned by the client (transferred or mirrored) with branch protection, CODEOWNERS and required checks configured for their team
- Terraform state in the client's account; backend documented; last plan shows no drift
- CI/CD running under the client's control (their CI or documented migration); pipeline roles via OIDC; no vendor secrets remain
- Artefact registry in the client's account with the deployed images; retention policy set
- Deployment records for every production deployment retained in the client's storage
- Dependency update automation configured and assigned to the client team
## 4. Access and credentials
- Credentials and access inventory complete: every secret, key, role, account, vendor login; owner and rotation schedule per item
- Every secret the vendor team handled rotated during handover; rotation evidence linked
- Client identities hold ownership of cloud accounts, DNS, registries, vendor consoles, monitoring, source control
- Vendor team access removed on the agreed date; removal verified by both sides (attempted access fails; IAM/IdP reports)
- Break-glass procedure documented and tested by the client
- Access review schedule set with a client owner
## 5. Security
- Production readiness and security review reports delivered with open items and accepted risks signed
- Penetration test report and remediation status delivered
- Security tooling (scanning, GuardDuty/Security Hub, WAF, SIEM integration) owned and monitored by the client
- Vulnerability management SLAs and the disclosure/reporting path agreed
- Data protection obligations and breach notification runbook handed over; contacts current
- Compliance evidence sources documented (where auditors look for each control)
## 6. Operations demonstrated by the receiving team
- Deploy a change through the pipeline to staging and production
- Roll back a deployment
- Respond to each paging alert from the runbook in a game day (bad deploy, dependency outage, certificate expiry at minimum)
- Rotate a secret and confirm the application recovers
- Restore the database from backup to a point in time and verify content
- Rebuild a non-production environment from IaC and the rebuild guide
- Grant and revoke access per the documented procedure
- Read the dashboards and explain the SLO status
- Locate every integration owner and vendor contact from the register
## 7. Monitoring, alerting and backups
- Alert destinations changed to the client's rota/distribution lists; test alert received by the client
- Dashboards provisioned in the client's tooling (or exported as code into their repository)
- Budget alarms sent to the client's finance and IT contacts
- Backups running to the client-owned, isolated vault; last restore test report within 30 days
- Synthetic probes and certificate expiry monitoring owned by the client
## 8. Knowledge transfer
- Walkthrough sessions recorded and linked (architecture, operations, codebase, security)
- Shadowing and reverse-shadowing completed for the core procedures
- Training record: who attended what; gaps and planned training noted
- Questions raised during transfer incorporated into documentation (issue list closed)
## 9. Commercial and closure
- Deliverables accepted against the statement of work
- Open change requests and backlog items transferred with priorities
- Licences, subscriptions and vendor contracts transferred or terminated; renewal dates with owners
- Data in vendor possession deleted (confirm no client data on vendor laptops, drives, chat tools, CI caches); deletion attestation signed
- Post-handover review meeting scheduled (30 days) and retrospective held internally
- Sign-off: client owner, receiving technical lead, vendor delivery lead, date
```

## If the receiving team cannot demonstrate section 6

Do not sign. Options (Part XIII chapter 12): extended hypercare with defined scope; hybrid or managed support with capability-building and exit criteria; targeted training before transfer; simplification of what is handed over; or an explicit, signed acceptance of the capability gap and its consequences by the client's sponsor. Document the choice and the plan.
